🧠 Part 3: The Explanation

1. The Closure (createHero)
We don't just use a simple variable like let level = 1. We wrap it in a function. 
This protects the data so it can only be changed by the up() and reset() methods. This is a core principle of Encapsulation.


2. The Map (.map())
Think of .map() as a transformer. It takes every monster in your array and applies the damage logic to them, 
returning a brand-new updated list.


3. The Filter (.filter())
This is the Janitor. After the map updates the HP, .filter() checks every monster. 
If their HP is not greater than 0, they are thrown away. This is how monsters "disappear" from your console.table.


4. The Reduce (.reduce())
This is the Cruncher. It looks at the whole array and "reduces" it down to one single number (the total HP). 
We use this number to determine the width of our green health bar.


5. The Find (.find())
This is the Scout. It ignores everyone else and looks for the first item that matches a specific name. 
If it finds it, we can display that specific monster's HP.


6. Persistence (localStorage)
By using localStorage.setItem and getItem, your Hero's level is stored in the browser's memory. 
Even if you close VS Code and come back tomorrow, your progress remains!
